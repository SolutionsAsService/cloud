# Critic: Provocateur (Debord) - R1

**Anchor used:** Debord, Society of the Spectacle, theses 1-2 and 18 (image replaces the thing; mediation replaces the lived), thesis 12 (the spectacle as "an enormous positivity" that demands only acceptance).

**Observed:** c3__1280.png: a tidy blue cloud, ten numbered lobes, handwritten labels outside, interior says only "the Internet". Article: the cloud's use is to "obscure what you don't know" (para 3); Gibson: its usefulness is its "vagueness" (para 12). c3__375.png: the cloud carries no words; meaning lives in an HTML list below.

**Findings:**
1. [claim] The page's implicit argument is that the cloud is a catalogue of ten reasons. That contradicts the article. A cloud whose purpose is vagueness is rendered as a labelled, equal-weight, numbered index in which nothing is unknown. The page does not admit this reversal, so the cloud illustrates the article without testing it.
2. [defect] The only enactment of the article's claim is the "your computer -> cloud -> the server" doodle with Cerf's "as long as it comes out of the cloud at the correct address". It sits in the cloud's footer, under the index, and not in the argument.
3. [defect] On phones the cloud is pure decoration; its lobes carry only numerals.
4. [premise] "10 answers" counts the authors' own offhand guesses as equal to testimony from Cerf and Taylor, and gives all ten identical lobes. The article's evidence is uneven; the cloud does not show that.

**Verdict:** OBJECT

**Requested change:** Make the cloud do what the article says it does. Show the vague interior concealing something, for example answers legible only at the cloud's edge, or the ten reasons drawn so they visibly obscure the Net. Or state the reversal on the page and prove it. On mobile, put the words inside the cloud.
